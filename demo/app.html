How to use this page

Each case is the sales history of one property or vehicle. Vestigium checks every sale with named rules and tells you whether the chain holds.

Pick a case

  • Property / Vehicle: made-up cases with planted problems (and some clean ones), so you can see each rule work.
  • Real: real public deeds from New York and Philadelphia, anonymised. Owners are numbered; no names or addresses.
  • Paste or upload: check your own documents (needs the local server).

Read the answer

  • Clean: every sale follows from the one before it.
  • Defective: a rule found a problem. The red box is the sale where it breaks.
  • Inconclusive: a record is missing. The amber box shows where, and nobody is accused.
  • Rests on a broken sale: a later sale that looks fine on its own, but whose chain of title passes through the broken one.

The panels, top to bottom

  1. Ownership chain: one box per sale, oldest on the left, with who sold, who bought, when it was signed and when it was recorded.
  2. Findings & proof: the rule that fired and the exact sentences of the records behind it. Minimal proof says how few records the finding needs.
  3. The graph itself: the same case as the database holds it. Run any rule's query, Walk the chain link by link, or remove a relationship to see the verdict change.
  4. Two kinds of time: legally true on picks which sales had been signed; known on picks which deeds had reached the public register. Move them together to stand in an investigator's shoes on that day, or apart to compare what was true with what could be known. The grid shows the verdict for every pair.
  5. AI agents (synthetic cases): an agent reading the documents alone, next to one that uses the rules. Replay it step by step.

Words

  • Grantor / grantee: seller / buyer.
  • Lien: a claim on the property, usually a loan, that should be released before a sale.
  • Power of attorney: permission for someone to sign for the owner, valid only between set dates.
  • Recorded: the date a deed reached the public register.

The AI never decides: the rules do, and the same records always give the same answer. Nothing here is legal advice.

Two kinds of time what had happened · what was on file

A sale is legally true from the day it is signed, but an investigator can only know about it once the deed reaches the public register, often weeks later. Vestigium keeps both dates for every fact, so it can answer either question.
